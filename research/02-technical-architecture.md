# Deep Technical Architecture Analysis: Viven, Delphi, and Tavus

## TL;DR
- **Three companies occupy three architecturally distinct layers of the "digital twin" stack.** Tavus is a full-stack, API-first *infrastructure* company that builds proprietary foundation models for real-time conversational video (Phoenix, Raven, Sparrow, Hummingbird); Delphi is a *consumer/creator* product built on a proprietary knowledge-graph "mind model" wrapping frontier LLMs (Llama 3.3 70B on Cerebras, plus OpenAI/Anthropic) with Pinecone RAG; Viven is an *enterprise-only* system built on a per-employee knowledge graph feeding a shared LLM, differentiated by "pairwise context and privacy."
- **None of the three trains a genuinely custom per-person neural network in production.** Delphi and Viven both use knowledge-graph + RAG grounding over shared/frontier LLMs (fine-tuning and RL are experimental, not core); only Tavus trains true proprietary foundation models — but those are for *rendering/perception/turn-taking*, not for a person's knowledge, which is handled by a bring-your-own-LLM layer plus RAG.
- **The real moats differ by layer:** Tavus's moat is hard real-time video research (sub-600ms Gaussian-diffusion rendering); Delphi's is a temporal knowledge-graph "mind model" plus scaled multi-tenant retrieval; Viven's is a privacy/authorization architecture (pairwise context) layered on knowledge-graph skills inference inherited from Eightfold.

## Key Findings
- **Tavus** builds four proprietary models — Phoenix-4 (Gaussian-diffusion real-time rendering), Raven-1 (multimodal perception), Sparrow-1 (audio-native turn-taking), Hummingbird-0 (zero-shot lip-sync) — orchestrated in a modular CVI pipeline over WebRTC (Daily). It is LLM-agnostic (bring-your-own or Tavus-hosted models) with RAG Knowledge Base and cross-session Memories.
- **Delphi** wraps frontier LLMs behind a proprietary "Digital Mind Architecture" (a self-adaptive temporal knowledge graph) with a Pinecone vector database (namespace-per-mind isolation), Cerebras for fast Llama 3.3 70B inference, AssemblyAI for transcription, ElevenLabs for voice, and Tavus for real-time video.
- **Viven** creates a per-employee digital twin from enterprise data (email, Slack, Google Docs, meetings) modeled as a dynamic knowledge graph, with a shared LLM "on top." Its differentiator is "pairwise context and privacy" — authorization-aware retrieval enforced at the knowledge-graph layer plus RBAC and audit trails — deployed on-prem or in the customer's cloud/VPC.

## Details

### 1. TAVUS — Infrastructure / API-first conversational video

**Core model architecture.** Tavus is the only one of the three that is fundamentally a *foundation-model research company* ("the human computing company"). Founded 2020 by Hassaan Raza and Quinn Favret (YC S21). Sequoia Capital led its $6.1M seed in March 2023; its most recent round was a **$40M Series B in November 2025 led by CRV** (with participation from Scale Venture Partners, Sequoia Capital, Y Combinator, HubSpot Ventures, and Flex Capital), bringing total funding to roughly $64M. Rather than a single model, Tavus ships a family of co-designed proprietary models:
- **Phoenix** (rendering): Phoenix-1 (2023) modeled avatars in 3D with NeRFs; Phoenix-2 switched to 3D Gaussian Splatting to break the real-time barrier; Phoenix-3 expanded generation from mouth/lips to the full face; **Phoenix-4** (Feb 2026) is a hybrid **Gaussian-diffusion** architecture trained on thousands of hours of conversational data, generating every pixel of head/shoulders including eye blinks as a full-duplex model that listens and speaks simultaneously. Pipeline: streaming audio feature extractor → long-term memory module → conditioning signals for a diffusion head. Phoenix-4 runs at **40fps in 1080p**.
- **Raven** (perception): Raven-0/Raven-1 is a multimodal perception model interpreting facial expressions, gaze, tone, emotion, and environmental context.
- **Sparrow** (turn-taking): Sparrow-1 is an audio-native transformer for conversational floor-transfer, operating at ~40ms frame-level granularity, modeling floor ownership probabilistically from prosodic/lexical/semantic cues rather than silence thresholds. Benchmarked against turn-taking systems on 28 challenging real-world conversational samples.
- **Hummingbird-0** (lip-sync): a zero-shot lip-sync model spun out of Phoenix-3 components; FID 63.92, LSE-D 6.74, Arcface 0.84 (Tavus claims ~37%/7%/7% better than closest competitors).

**Data ingestion / knowledge pipeline.** For the *person*, Tavus needs remarkably little: a replica can be trained from as little as ~1-2 minutes of source video. Separately, its **Knowledge Base** is a RAG system: documents (.pdf/.docx/.txt/images/.pptx/.csv/.xlsx) or crawled websites are uploaded, processed in the background (5-10 min), and retrieved during conversation with a configurable strategy (`speed`/`balanced`/`quality`). Tavus markets Knowledge Base retrieval in as little as 30ms. **Memories** provide cross-session recall.

**Voice/video/multimodal stack.** This is Tavus's core. The CVI pipeline runs, in order: Perception (Raven) → Conversational Flow (Sparrow) → STT → LLM → TTS → Realtime replica (Phoenix). Media transport is **WebRTC, powered by Daily** (Daily.co hosted; Pipecat/LiveKit integrations available for custom transport). TTS is pluggable: Cartesia (default), ElevenLabs, or Azure. It supports 1080p video, 24kHz audio, and 42+ languages. Phoenix-4 targets **sub-600ms end-to-end latency** (Tavus's homepage separately markets "<500ms") with a "stream-first" architecture (incremental WebRTC video packets rather than full-file render).

**Personalization/memory.** Persona behavior/tone/knowledge is defined via the "PAL" (behavior layer) mapped onto a "Face" (visual). Memories + Knowledge Base + Objectives + Guardrails compose the personalization layer. Phoenix-4 adds an Emotion Control API (joy/sadness/anger/surprise) that adjusts facial geometry (e.g., genuine zygomatic engagement for joy).

**Infrastructure/deployment.** API-first: React components, iframe, Daily SDK, server-to-server. LLM layer supports Tavus-hosted models (e.g., tavus-gpt-oss, tavus-gemini, tavus-claude-haiku, and larger frontier models) or any OpenAI-compatible streaming endpoint including Azure OpenAI. Enterprise plan offers white-labeling; SOC 2, HIPAA, GDPR options. Uses Cerebras for fast inference (named as a Cerebras customer). Customers include CVS, Mayo Clinic, and others.

**Differentiation/moats.** Tavus's claim is that real-time emotionally intelligent conversational video cannot be assembled from commodity APIs — its four proprietary models with published benchmarks are the moat. It explicitly positions as the single integration point replacing a 5-6 vendor stack.

**Published technical material.** Tavus maintains a research site and detailed engineering blog posts for Phoenix-4, Raven-1, Sparrow-1, and Hummingbird-0, plus a Sparrow-1 Hacker News "Show HN" thread; full developer docs at docs.tavus.io (including llms.txt / llms-full.txt / openapi.yaml).

### 2. DELPHI — Consumer/creator "digital minds"

**Core model architecture.** Delphi (delphi.ai; founded Nov 2022 by Dara Ladjevardian and Sam Spelsberg) does **not** train its own foundation models. It raised a **$16M Series A led by Sequoia Capital in June 2025** (following a $2.7M seed, ~$18.7M total; other investors include Menlo Ventures, Anthropic's Anthology Fund, and Founders Fund). It wraps frontier LLMs behind a proprietary orchestration layer it calls the **"Clone Brain" / "Digital Mind Architecture (DMA1)"** — described as a "high-throughput, temporally indexed, self-adaptive temporal knowledge graph" inspired by Ray Kurzweil's *How to Create a Mind* (mind as hierarchical pattern recognizers). It layers explicit knowledge (what you know) with implicit reasoning (how you think). Content is embedded using models "from OpenAI, Anthropic, or Delphi's own stack." Crucially, Delphi does **not** train its models on user content / does not pool user data — a deliberate IP-protection stance for creators.

**Data ingestion / knowledge pipeline.** RAG pipeline: content (YouTube, Notion, blogs, social, books, PDFs, Google Drive, podcasts) is ingested, cleaned, chunked, embedded, and stored in **Pinecone**, one **namespace per Digital Mind**. AssemblyAI transcribes audio/video (the majority of uploaded content), with multilingual speaker identification. At query time the query is transformed and sent to Pinecone; per Pinecone's case study, Delphi "supports more than 100 million vectors across 12,000+ namespaces" with real-time vector search "consistently return[ing] results in under 100ms at P95" (accounting for <30% of the 1-second end-to-end target), at ~20 queries/second globally with "zero scaling incidents." Delphi targets scaling to 5M+ namespaces. **Interview Mode / Interviewer** lets an agent ask the creator structured questions to fill knowledge gaps — lowering the data barrier for people without large archives.

**Voice/video/multimodal stack.** Delphi is text/voice/video but **integrates third parties** rather than building its own: **ElevenLabs** for voice cloning (30-60s sample; Pro-Voice add-on), and **Tavus** for real-time video ("FaceTime with your AI twin"). Per Tavus's own case study, Delphi chose Tavus for photorealistic AI humans with accurate lip-sync integrating with Delphi's existing ElevenLabs voice engine at sub-1-second latency, launching with 125 AI humans and ~2-second live-call latency / HD video.

**Personalization/memory.** The temporal knowledge graph captures how a person's views evolve over time and their reasoning patterns/associations, not just facts. Strict mode guardrails prevent responses outside trained content. Delphi is hiring "Applied AI Engineers" to improve knowledge-graph architectures, novel embeddings, and tone/style transfer — signaling that persona-tone matching is still an active R&D area beyond RAG.

**Infrastructure/deployment.** Consumer SaaS with deployment across SMS, WhatsApp, Zoom, Slack, Discord, personal websites. Inference: **Cerebras** serving **Llama 3.3 70B** — per Cerebras's case study, "the greatest unlock for Delphi is that Cerebras serves state of the art models – Llama 3.3 70B – at the fastest speeds on earth" (Cerebras benchmarks that model at ~2,100 tokens/second; Delphi cited ~2x tokens/sec vs other providers, up to 5-10x vs GPU providers). Pinecone serverless (SOC 2, encryption, namespace isolation, one-API-call deletion). Serves 2,000+ creators (e.g., Matthew Hussey, Lenny Rachitsky, Jason Lemkin).

**Differentiation/moats.** Delphi's stated moat is the DMA1 temporal knowledge graph ("not flat like traditional RAG") plus verified-identity authenticity and creator data ownership. Its CEO argues (via Sequoia) that distribution and product experience — not early technical moats — win consumer markets.

**Published technical material.** Founder podcast/interview with Sequoia's *Training Data*; Cerebras customer engineering blog; Pinecone customer case study (detailed retrieval architecture); VentureBeat deep-dive on the Pinecone scaling; developer docs at docs.delphi.ai.

### 3. VIVEN — Enterprise-only employee digital twins

**Core model architecture.** Viven (viven.ai; legally WisdomLabs.AI Inc.; founded 2025 by Ashutosh Garg and Varun Kacholia, ex-Eightfold AI). Per PR Newswire, "Viven today emerged from stealth with $35 million in seed funding from Khosla Ventures, Foundation Capital, FPV Ventures, Operator Collective, and leading angel investors" on October 15, 2025. Despite press describing "a customized LLM for each employee," the CEO's own more precise description is a **shared LLM layered on top of a per-employee, dynamically generated knowledge graph** — i.e., knowledge-graph-grounded RAG, not genuinely separate fine-tuned weights per person. Garg told diginomica: "There is an LLM on top that simplifies the process of asking questions and summarizing data. But the context fed into the LLM is limited to what can be pulled out of the knowledge graph." Per-employee **reinforcement learning and fine-tuning are explicitly experimental** ("we are conducting various experiments right now with reinforcement learning and fine-tuning as well"), not confirmed production. The specific underlying foundation model (GPT/Claude/Gemini/Llama/open-weight) is **not publicly disclosed**.

**Data ingestion / knowledge pipeline.** Each twin ingests an employee's real work data — emails, Slack/chat threads, Google Docs, internal documents, meeting notes/transcripts — across enterprise apps (Gmail, Outlook, Drive, SharePoint, Slack, Teams, Jira, Confluence, Salesforce, GitHub, Box, Zoom). The distinctive design is a **knowledge graph** that distills "weak signals" of knowledge, experience, and expertise (a direct lineage from Eightfold's "skills inference") and captures how expertise evolves over time. The LLM only receives context retrievable from the knowledge graph — a deliberate hallucination-limiting design. Team-level twins aggregate whole departments.

**Voice/video/multimodal stack.** Viven is essentially **text-based enterprise Q&A** — no voice cloning or video avatar; Garg says the focus is "on knowledge, experiences, expertise, less on tone tonality." This is the sharpest contrast with Delphi and Tavus.

**Personalization/memory.** Context modeling per person includes *why* a question is being asked and prior conversation history between the two parties. This flows into the headline **"pairwise context and privacy"** system: responses are tailored to the requester-twin relationship (a VP answers "How's the quarter going?" differently for the CEO vs a board member), enforced via authorization-aware retrieval, granular RBAC, and full audit trails (every query is visible to the twin's owner, a social deterrent). Personal-life or confidential data (PII, credit-card numbers) is withheld. Ashu Garg, GP at Foundation Capital, told TechCrunch of the underlying problem: "It's a very hard problem to solve, and until recently, it was unsolvable."

**Infrastructure/deployment.** Deployment is on-premises, in the customer's private VPC/BYOC, or hosted SaaS — with data isolation, encryption, and full customer control. The **specific cloud provider for Viven's own hosted stack (AWS/GCP/Azure) is not publicly disclosed**, and no SOC 2/FedRAMP certification is publicly named (Garg alluded to being mid-way through "a major certification"). Deployed at Genpact — which per Pulse 2.0/PR Newswire "implemented Viven's platform across its executive leadership team within eight weeks," with Genpact CEO Balkrishan "BK" Kalra crediting it with the "organizational velocity to collaborate with clarity, speed and confidence" — as well as Eightfold, Prodapt, Red Crackle, and The Josh Bersin Company.

**Differentiation/moats.** Viven's claimed moat is the pairwise privacy/authorization architecture plus decades of knowledge-graph expertise from the founders (Garg's work on entity disambiguation at Google, Bloomreach, and skills inference at Eightfold). TechCrunch notes the pairwise approach as a potential moat while flagging that hyperscalers (Anthropic, Google's Gemini, Microsoft Copilot, and OpenAI's enterprise-search products) could enter this space.

**Published technical material.** No engineering blog, whitepaper, or patent detailing the pairwise-context algorithm is public. The most technical primary source is the diginomica interview with Garg; other coverage (TechCrunch, SiliconANGLE, VentureBeat) is largely derivative of the launch.

## Comparative Technical Summary

| Dimension | Tavus | Delphi | Viven |
|---|---|---|---|
| Category | Infrastructure / API | Consumer / creator product | Enterprise-only |
| Trains own foundation models? | **Yes** (Phoenix/Raven/Sparrow/Hummingbird — for video/perception/turn-taking) | No (wraps frontier LLMs) | No (shared LLM + knowledge graph) |
| Knowledge/persona approach | Bring-your-own LLM + RAG Knowledge Base + Memories | Temporal knowledge graph (DMA1) + Pinecone RAG | Dynamic per-employee knowledge graph + RAG |
| Vector DB | Internal RAG | **Pinecone** (namespace-per-mind) | Knowledge-graph-centric (vector DB not disclosed) |
| LLM inference | Tavus-hosted or OpenAI-compatible | **Cerebras / Llama 3.3 70B** + OpenAI/Anthropic | Undisclosed shared LLM |
| Voice/video | **Proprietary real-time video** (WebRTC/Daily) | ElevenLabs (voice) + **Tavus** (video) | Text only |
| Latency posture | Sub-600ms end-to-end video (40fps/1080p) | <100ms P95 retrieval; ~1s target; ~2s live video | Not disclosed |
| Deployment | Cloud API, white-label | Consumer SaaS across channels | On-prem / VPC / BYOC |
| Key moat | Real-time rendering research | Temporal mind model + scaled retrieval | Pairwise privacy/authorization |

The three are more *complementary than competitive*: Delphi is literally a Tavus customer (video) and an ElevenLabs customer (voice), while Viven addresses a different buyer (enterprise IT/knowledge management) with a text-first, privacy-first design. The clearest architectural throughline is that "digital twin" fidelity today is achieved less by training bespoke models of a person and more by **grounding shared/frontier models in per-person structured knowledge (knowledge graphs + RAG)** — with the differentiation living in the retrieval/privacy layer (Viven), the knowledge-graph "mind model" (Delphi), or the real-time multimodal rendering stack (Tavus).

## Recommendations
- **If you need to embed a real-time interactive AI persona (video/voice) in a product:** Use **Tavus** as infrastructure. It is the only one of the three with genuine proprietary real-time video research and a bring-your-own-LLM design. Benchmark against its sub-600ms claim in your own network conditions; validate the 30ms Knowledge Base retrieval and Memories for your use case. Threshold to reconsider: if you only need scripted/pre-rendered video, a generation tool (HeyGen/Synthesia) is cheaper.
- **If you are a creator/expert monetizing knowledge to an audience:** Use **Delphi**. Its knowledge-graph + Pinecone + Cerebras stack is purpose-built for creator "digital minds" with data-ownership guarantees. Validate the tone/style fidelity yourself (it is still an active R&D area) and lean on Interview Mode if you lack a large content archive.
- **If your goal is internal enterprise knowledge continuity/collaboration:** Evaluate **Viven**, but demand specifics it has not published: the underlying foundation model, the hosted-cloud provider, SOC 2/other certifications, and a technical description of pairwise-context enforcement. Because hyperscaler copilots could replicate this, treat the pairwise-privacy moat as the key due-diligence item. Threshold: if your org already runs Microsoft Copilot/Google Gemini enterprise and privacy needs are modest, the incremental value may be limited.
- **Cross-cutting:** For any of the three, the decisive technical due-diligence question is *"where does persona fidelity actually come from?"* — in all three cases the answer is primarily retrieval/knowledge-graph grounding over shared models, so evaluate retrieval quality, latency, and privacy isolation rather than assuming a bespoke per-person model.

## Caveats
- **Marketing vs. architecture:** Both Viven's "customized LLM for each employee" and Delphi's "3D model of your mind" are marketing framings; the underlying reality in both is knowledge-graph-grounded RAG over shared/frontier LLMs, with fine-tuning/RL experimental. I have flagged this explicitly where sources allow.
- **Undisclosed specifics:** Viven's foundation model and cloud provider are not public; Delphi's exact embedding models and full LLM routing are only partially disclosed; Tavus's internal LLM identities are partly branded ("tavus-gpt-oss" etc.).
- **Source quality:** Tavus and Delphi have genuine technical/engineering sources (research blogs, Pinecone/Cerebras case studies, docs). Viven has essentially one technical primary source (the diginomica interview); most other Viven coverage is derivative of its launch PR. Several Phoenix-4/latency figures come from Tavus's own materials and vendor benchmarks and should be independently validated.
- **Dates/figures:** Funding totals and model versions are current as of early 2026 and may have changed. Latency and benchmark numbers are vendor-reported unless otherwise noted. Note there are two distinct people named "Ashu/Ashutosh Garg" in the Viven story — Ashutosh Garg (Viven CEO/co-founder) and Ashu Garg (GP at Foundation Capital, an investor).
