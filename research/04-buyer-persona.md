# Who Buys Viven: The Buyer Persona for Enterprise AI "Digital Twins"

## TL;DR
- The main buyer is the CEO and C-suite executive office (CEO, Chief of Staff, and transformation-minded leaders), with enterprise IT as the mandatory approver, not the CHRO. Despite the founders' HR-tech pedigree from Eightfold, Viven positions itself as a horizontal, CEO-level "run the enterprise" tool, and every named customer champion is a CEO, Chief of Staff, CIO/Transformation Officer, CGO, or CFO.
- The clearest deployment pattern (Genpact, "across its global leadership" in eight weeks) shows the buying center is the executive leadership team plus IT/security, sponsored top-down by the CEO or Chief of Staff, with employees as end users and Legal/Privacy/security as blockers due to employee-consent issues.
- This differs from HR-owned tools (Sana/Workday) and IT-owned tools (Moveworks/ServiceNow, Glean). Viven's buyer is converging on the CEO office plus IT rather than HR, even though HR analysts (Josh Bersin) are its loudest cheerleaders.

## Key Findings
- Viven's own language targets executives and "leaders," not HR. CEO Ashutosh Garg: "Digital Twins give executives immediate access to the thinking, context, and decisions of their entire organization." The launch release frames the pain around the CEO: "every CEO faces the same existential challenge."
- Every testimonial on viven.ai comes from a CEO, Chief of Staff, CIO/Transformation Officer, Chief Growth Officer, or CFO. There is no CHRO or Chief People Officer testimonial, which is striking given the Eightfold lineage.
- The flagship deployment, Genpact (146,500 employees as of 2025), rolled out "across its global leadership" in eight weeks, and the champions quoted are the CEO (BK Kalra), Chief of Staff to the CEO (Brian Baral), CIO and Transformation Officer (Vidya Rao), and an enterprise-architecture/digital-ops leader (Munish Dargan). This is a CEO-office plus IT buying center, not HR.
- Investor framing confirms IT as the approver. Foundation Capital: the founders "know the difference between a compelling demo and a product that IT departments will approve and employees will use."
- The ICP is large, global, distributed enterprises with knowledge workers, entered top-down at the leadership tier and then expanded. Viven also offers a three-tier funnel (For Individuals via AskSila, For Businesses self-serve signup, For Enterprise book-a-demo), but the paying enterprise motion is founder-led, high-touch, multi-stakeholder sales.
- Privacy and consent pull in specific approvers/blockers: Legal, Privacy, security review, and (in Europe) potentially works councils, because building AI twins of employees from their emails, chats, and documents raises consent, ownership, and data-protection questions.

## Details

### 1. Economic buyer: CEO office plus IT, not HR
Viven's marketing and founder statements point to the CEO and senior executives as the economic buyer and primary beneficiary. CEO Ashutosh Garg frames the value at the executive level: "Digital Twins give executives immediate access to the thinking, context, and decisions of their entire organization so they can move faster, see across silos, and stay ahead of the curve." The stealth-exit release opens with the CEO's problem: "every CEO faces the same existential challenge: AI isn't just a technology trend, it's a competitive reckoning."

The company does not publish a "For HR leaders" page or a "For IT" page. Instead, the homepage segments by user type and company size (For Individuals, For Businesses, For Enterprise) and rotates a "Built for Engineering / Sales / You" headline, positioning by function and by the individual employee rather than naming HR or IT as the buyer. The enterprise-readiness section speaks directly to IT and security: on-premises, private VPC, or hosted SaaS deployment, role-based access controls, compliance, and audit trails.

Enterprise IT is the approver/integration gatekeeper. Foundation Capital's Ashu Garg wrote that the founders know how "to get big organizations to adopt new technology... a product that IT departments will approve and employees will use." This is a strong signal that technical approval sits with IT even if the budget and vision sit with the CEO office.

Verdict: the economic buyer is the CEO or executive office (often via a Chief of Staff or a transformation/AI leader), with IT as co-approver. HR is not the budget owner on current evidence.

### 2. Testimonials reveal the buying and sponsoring titles
The customer voices Viven chose to feature map cleanly to a CEO-office plus IT/transformation buying center:
- Balkrishan "BK" Kalra, President and CEO, Genpact (executive sponsor): "Speed and agility are fundamental elements of our culture... which gives our leadership team the organizational velocity to collaborate with clarity, speed, and confidence."
- Brian Baral, Chief of Staff to the CEO of Genpact (champion and power user; used a predecessor's twin for role handover).
- Vidya Rao, Chief Information and Transformation Officer, Genpact (IT/transformation approver; "must-have for the future of work").
- Munish Dargan, Leader Technology Strategy, Enterprise Architecture and Enabling Digital Ops, Genpact (IT/architecture evaluator).
- Sumit Grover, Chief Growth Officer, Prodapt (line-of-business/growth champion).
- Celia Poon, CFO of Eightfold (ROI/economic framing: "faster decisions, fewer tools, less support overhead").
- Josh Bersin and Bill Pelster of The Josh Bersin Company (analyst endorsement; both quoted as founder/CEO users).
- Neerav Mehta, Founder and CEO, Redcrackle (SMB CEO champion).

The pattern: CEO sponsorship, Chief of Staff as day-to-day champion, CIO/transformation and enterprise architecture as approvers, CFO as ROI validator, and CGO/line-of-business as expansion champions. No HR buyer appears.

### 3. GTM signals: hiring, events, analysts
Hiring: Viven's only observable go-to-market role is a "Strategic AI Enterprise Account Executive" (Santa Clara, on-site). The job description is revealing about the sales motion but silent on buyer title. It calls for building "pipeline from scratch through outbound, referrals, events... there is no SDR layer," owning "complex, multi-stakeholder enterprise deals," educating "buyers on a new category (AI-powered Digital Twins)," navigating "procurement, legal, and security reviews typical of enterprise sales cycles," and defining "ICP, messaging, and sales approach." It requires selling "technical products and engaging both business and technical stakeholders." This describes a founder-led, category-creation, multi-stakeholder enterprise sale, with legal and security as explicit gates, and confirms the ICP is not yet fixed. No CHRO or HR-tech selling experience is requested, which would be expected if HR were the target buyer.

Analysts and events: The loudest analyst voice is Josh Bersin, an HR industry analyst, who uses Viven and hosted Garg on his podcast. This is an HR-flavored signal, but it reflects Bersin's personal thought leadership on "digital twins" and "superworkers" more than a Viven decision to sell into HR. Notably, Bersin himself is positioned as a CEO/user, and his firm's HR 2030 work treats digital twins as a workforce trend, not as an HR-owned procurement. I found no evidence Viven sponsors HR Tech, UNLEASH, SHRM, or Gartner ReimagineHR, nor developer/IT conferences. Media pitches skew to general business and tech (WSJ, BBC, TechCrunch, diginomica, Inc.), consistent with a CEO/executive audience rather than a functional HR audience.

### 4. Founder and investor statements
Garg repeatedly casts the CEO and executives as the users and beneficiaries, and gives examples of a CEO querying a CFO's twin. He claims no direct competitor "is tackling digital twins for the enterprise yet." Foundation Capital's thesis is explicitly horizontal: "there's this horizontal problem across all jobs of coordination and communication, which no one is automating," and the aim is for Viven to "become the default interface for knowledge and action across every company, as ubiquitous as Slack and email." Khosla frames it at the organizational level: "By creating a digital twin for every employee, Viven gives organizations the ability to move faster." None of the principals frames HR as the buyer. Operator Collective and the Eightfold lineage both skew toward HR/talent networks, which likely aids warm introductions, but the stated target is the enterprise leadership team and IT.

No source discloses average deal or contract size, or total customer count. Named customers/deployments are Genpact, Eightfold, The Josh Bersin Company, Prodapt, and Redcrackle. Deal size is therefore unknown from public evidence.

### 5. Company size and segment
The ICP is large, global enterprises with distributed knowledge workers and cross-time-zone coordination costs, entered top-down at the leadership tier. Genpact (146,500 employees as of 2025) is the lighthouse account, deployed "across its global leadership" in eight weeks (per the Oct 15, 2025 launch release: "In just eight weeks, Genpact... deployed Viven's Digital Twins across its global leadership, improving internal collaboration, and ensuring uninterrupted decision-making"). The self-serve "For Businesses" tier (digitaltwin.viven.ai/signup) and the consumer "For Individuals" AskSila product suggest a product-led on-ramp and a possible future mid-market/SMB motion, but the paying, referenceable business today is large-enterprise, executive-sponsored. Self-serve pricing is not public; Viven runs a subscription plus free-trial model per its terms of service, with no dollar figures disclosed.

### 6. Buying center roles (distinguished)
- Economic buyer (signs the check): CEO or executive office; in practice often released through a Chief of Staff, COO, or a Chief Digital/AI/Transformation officer who holds transformation budget. Not HR on current evidence.
- Champion (drives it internally): Chief of Staff to the CEO (Genpact's Brian Baral is the archetype), or a CEO directly; secondarily line-of-business leaders (CGO).
- Technical approver: CIO/CTO, Chief Information and Transformation Officer, enterprise architecture, and security, who vet deployment (VPC/on-prem), RBAC, and audit trails.
- Economic validator: CFO (Eightfold's CFO gives the ROI testimonial).
- End users: employees whose twins are created, and employees who query colleagues' twins.
- Blockers/approvers: Legal, Privacy, and security review, and potentially works councils in the EU, because employee twins raise consent, IP ownership (who owns a departed employee's twin), and data-protection questions. Bersin explicitly flags "lots of privacy issues" and the legal question of who owns a twin left behind. Employment lawyers flag consent, likeness, confidentiality, and post-employment use.

The buying center for "employee twins" is not converging cleanly on HR. Tools framed as employee support and service (Moveworks, Glean) sit with IT; learning and HR-workflow tools (Sana) sit with HR; Viven deliberately sits higher, at the CEO/leadership level, with IT as approver. Industry analysts note the broader trend that AI adoption is forcing HR-IT "fusion": Gartner predicts "30% of organizations will form blended HR-IT teams by 2029 to accelerate AI enablement" (Gartner, Aug 2025; VP Analyst Mark Whittle). But for Viven specifically the observable owner today is the executive office plus IT.

## Recommendations
- If you are selling to, partnering with, or competing against Viven, treat the CEO office (CEO plus Chief of Staff) as the economic buyer and primary champion, and plan for a parallel IT/security approval track and a Legal/Privacy consent track. Bring a CFO-facing ROI case early.
- Target ICP: global enterprises with large, distributed knowledge workforces and high coordination costs (consulting, IT services, BPO, global product orgs). Genpact is the template.
- Lead with a top-down, leadership-team pilot (the Genpact eight-week pattern) rather than a bottom-up functional HR pilot. Land at the leadership tier, then expand to line-of-business (sales, customer success, engineering) via team twins.
- Prepare consent and governance answers up front: individual and admin privacy controls, query auditability, RBAC, VPC/on-prem deployment, and a clear position on twin ownership after an employee departs. These are the deal blockers.
- Benchmarks that would change this read: if Viven begins hiring GTM staff with explicit "HR tech" or "sell to CHRO" mandates, sponsors HR Tech/UNLEASH/SHRM, publishes a "For HR leaders" page, or its testimonials start featuring CHROs, then the buyer is shifting toward HR. Conversely, hiring security/compliance sales engineers and publishing SOC 2/ISO and DPA collateral would confirm the IT-plus-CEO center. Watch also for disclosed per-seat pricing, which would signal a scaled mid-market motion.

## Caveats
- Most evidence is first-party (Viven's site, its Oct 15, 2025 stealth-exit release, founder interviews) or investor commentary, which reflects marketed positioning, not necessarily where budget actually sits. The only third-party deployment detail (Genpact eight weeks, "across global leadership") traces back to the press release, so treat the specifics as company-sourced.
- Viven emerged from stealth on Oct 15, 2025 with a $35M seed round (Khosla Ventures, Foundation Capital, FPV Ventures, Operator Collective, and angels); it is early and post-seed, and its ICP is explicitly still being defined (its own AE job says so), so the buyer persona is a moving target and may broaden or shift toward HR or IT as it scales.
- No public data on deal size, contract structure, self-serve pricing, or total customers. The founder's "no competitors" claim is debatable given adjacent players (Glean, Sana, Moveworks, IgniteTech, Sensay).
- The Josh Bersin association is a genuine HR-analyst signal and should not be dismissed; it could presage an HR-oriented go-to-market even though current buying centers are executive/IT.

Rough token estimate: about 2,700 tokens.
