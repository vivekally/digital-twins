Key signals from the scorecard

Platform AI is the existential threat. Microsoft 365 Copilot (7.5), ChatGPT Memory (7.0), and Claude Projects (7.0) ship context as a native property of the model. When the tool you already use adds memory, a standalone company brain needs a defensible wedge — not just “we also do knowledge.”

Notion AI still leads company brain (8.2) because it ships the broadest capability set in one surface. But the YC 2026 cohort is coming fast: Hyper (6.9), Cerenovus (6.5), Memory Store (6.5), Savant (6.2), and Glen (6.3) all ship MCP-native company brains with knowledge graphs. The race is to become the context layer AI agents query by default.

GBrain (7.5) is the open-source dark horse. Markdown-first, local-first, MIT licensed with 28K stars. Perfect 10 on privacy, export, and pricing. Its “dream cycle” that consolidates memory overnight is a novel architecture no proprietary tool has replicated.

Obsidian (7.5) still leads personal brain on sovereignty. But the gap is closing — Mem.ai (a16z-backed, 6.6) and Reflect (6.6) ship AI-native experiences that Obsidian achieves only through plugins.

Memory infra is an emerging layer. Quivr (YC W24, 6.9), Mem0 (6.7, 63K stars), and Screenpipe (YC S26, 6.5) are building the memory primitives that company brains and personal brains will consume. LangChain/LlamaIndex (6.6) remain the orchestration default.

Enterprise incumbents are being acquired. Moveworks → ServiceNow ($2.85B), Sana → Workday, Dashworks → HubSpot, Starmind → Capacity. The standalone enterprise knowledge category is consolidating into platform suites.

15 Dimensions

What we test, and how. Each dimension targets a specific capability that a second brain should deliver.

01
AI Search & Retrieval
Test: Ask 10 natural-language questions across stored knowledge. Score relevance, citation accuracy, and “I don’t know” calibration.
Quality of semantic search, Q&A over your knowledge base, and the ability to surface relevant information you forgot you had.
02
AI Synthesis
Test: Feed 20 notes on related topics. Ask for a summary that connects themes. Score insight quality and source attribution.
Ability to summarize, connect ideas across notes, generate insights, and produce derivative content (audio overviews, study guides, briefings).
03
Knowledge Capture
Test: Capture content from 5 sources (web clip, file import, quick note, API, paste). Score speed, fidelity, and metadata preservation.
Breadth of capture methods: web clippers, file imports, email forwarding, API ingestion, quick-capture, and ambient capture (screen, audio, meetings).
04
Integrations
Test: Connect to 5 external tools (Slack, email, calendar, CRM, code repos). Score connector depth and bidirectionality.
Native and third-party integrations. Slack, Teams, Gmail, Google Drive, GitHub, Jira, Salesforce, Zapier, and the ability to pull context from where work actually happens.
05
Graph & Linking
Test: Create 30 notes with cross-references. Verify backlinks, graph visualization, and surfaced connections. Score discoverability.
Bidirectional links, knowledge graph visualization, backlinks, block references, and the ability to see relationships between ideas that you didn’t explicitly create.
06
AI Writing Assist
Test: Draft a document from notes. Use AI to edit, expand, translate, and change tone. Score quality, context-awareness, and style fidelity.
AI-powered drafting, editing, rewriting, summarization, and tone adjustment. Context-aware completion that draws on your own knowledge, not just general LLM output.
07
Privacy & Security
Test: Check encryption (at rest, in transit, E2E), compliance certs, data residency, and local-first options. Score sovereignty.
E2E encryption, local-first storage, SOC 2 / ISO 27001, GDPR / CCPA, data residency, on-device processing, and the right to delete.
08
Cross-Platform
Test: Use the product on desktop, mobile, web, and offline. Score parity, sync speed, and offline capability.
Desktop (Mac, Windows, Linux), mobile (iOS, Android), web, and offline access. Feature parity across platforms and sync reliability.
09
Collaboration
Test: Share a workspace with 3 people. Test real-time co-editing, comments, permissions, and conflict resolution.
Real-time co-editing, comments, @mentions, sharing controls, team spaces, and permission granularity. The difference between “my brain” and “our brain.”
10
API & Extensibility
Test: Build a custom integration using the published API. Score documentation, endpoint coverage, plugin ecosystem, and developer experience.
REST/GraphQL APIs, plugin ecosystems, community themes, webhook support, automation (Zapier, Make), and the ability to extend the product beyond its defaults.
11
Export & Portability
Test: Export all content. Score format quality (Markdown, JSON, HTML), structure preservation, and re-importability elsewhere.
Data portability and lock-in risk. Standard export formats, bulk export, file-based storage, and the ability to leave without losing structure or metadata.
12
Media Handling
Test: Attach a PDF, image, audio, and video file. Score rendering, search indexing, OCR, and inline preview quality.
PDFs, images (OCR), audio (transcription), video (embeds), drawings, scans, and the ability to search inside non-text media.
13
Setup Speed
Test: Time from zero to first productive note. Include account creation, onboarding, and initial content import.
How quickly a new user becomes productive. Instant (built-in, zero config), minutes (self-serve SaaS), hours (import + configure), or days (enterprise deployment).
14
Freshness & Sync
Test: Edit on device A, check device B. Measure sync latency. Test conflict resolution on simultaneous edits.
Real-time sync across devices, auto-save, conflict resolution, and how quickly changes propagate. Includes ambient capture freshness for tools like Limitless.
15
Pricing Value
Test: Evaluate free tier usefulness, paid plan cost, and features per dollar. Score accessibility for individuals and small teams.
Free tier quality, subscription cost, one-time purchase options, per-seat vs flat pricing, and total cost of ownership including storage and AI credits.

How to read scores

10 best-in-class 8 strong 6 adequate 4 weak 2 poor — not tested N/A doesn’t apply

Overall is the mean of scored dimensions only. Untested (—) and N/A dimensions are excluded from the mean. A product with 5/15 tested and a 9.0 overall is not “better” than one with 15/15 tested and a 7.0 — the tested fraction matters.

How scoring works

One scale, open criteria, vendor-reported data. Independent testing is the goal; this version is a starting framework.

Scale

Every dimension is scored 1–10 after evaluating the product against published criteria. Scores are assigned based on public documentation, product demos, engineering blogs, customer case studies, and press coverage. No score comes from private access or paid testing.

9–10 Best-in-class. Sets the standard for the category.
7–8 Strong. Competitive, with clear evidence of capability.
5–6 Adequate. Functional but with notable gaps or missing evidence.
3–4 Weak. Present but limited. Significant improvement needed.
1–2 Poor. Barely functional or fundamentally missing.

Overall calculation

Overall = mean of all scored dimensions. Untested (—) and N/A dimensions are excluded. This means a product with fewer tested dimensions may show a higher overall if its tested areas are strong. Always read Overall alongside the Tested fraction.

A product scoring 7.5 on 13/15 dimensions tells you more than one scoring 8.5 on 3/15.

Category definitions

Platform AI: Model vendors (Microsoft, OpenAI, Anthropic) that ship context and memory as native features of their AI platform. They control the model and distribution — the most dangerous competitive position.

Company Brain: Products whose primary use case is team knowledge management — shared wikis, enterprise search, internal knowledge bases, and AI-powered answers across organizational data. Includes incumbents and YC/a16z-backed pure-plays.

Enterprise Knowledge: Funded incumbents and work AI platforms that serve enterprise knowledge as part of a larger platform (support automation, GTM, DevOps). Many have been acquired by suite vendors.

Personal Brain: Products designed for individual knowledge management — personal note-taking, knowledge graphs, life capture, and AI-assisted thinking. Optimized for privacy, portability, and personal workflow.

Memory Infra: The infrastructure layer — agent memory frameworks, RAG platforms, and capture tools that other products build on. Often open-source with strong developer communities.

Why “Second Brain”?

The term comes from Tiago Forte’s Building a Second Brain methodology: an external system that captures, organizes, and resurfaces knowledge so you don’t have to hold it all in your head. Every product in this benchmark aspires to that — whether for one person or one thousand.

A Company Brain is the organizational version: institutional memory that survives employee turnover, answers questions from anyone, and connects scattered knowledge across teams and tools.

A Personal Brain is the individual version: your notes, highlights, recordings, and ideas, linked and searchable, amplified by AI.

Data sources & caveats

Scores are derived from: company websites, product documentation, engineering blogs, press releases, analyst coverage, funding announcements, customer case studies, community forums, and live product demos where accessible.

Key caveats:

• All data is vendor-reported unless noted. No independent lab testing has been performed.

• AI features in this category are evolving weekly. Scores reflect the state as of September 2026.

• “Not tested” (—) means insufficient data to score, not that the capability is absent.

• Plugin and community ecosystem scores change as communities grow or shrink.

• Pricing can change without notice; scores reflect published pricing at time of evaluation.
