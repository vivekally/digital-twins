01 Market Aug 2026

Three markets are wearing one name.

Every "digital twin market size" headline blends a factory-simulation business, a video-avatar business, and a replica-of-a-named-person business. Only the first two have revenue anyone measures. The category this report is about, a conversational twin of a specific human, has no analyst coverage at all.

A · Asset & process twins $26–40B

2026 market, seven research firms · 30–42% CAGR

Factories, grids, supply chains, and now whole organizations. Gartner published its first-ever Magic Quadrant for Digital Twin of an Organization platforms in 2026; Celonis and SAP named Leaders. This is where the headline dollars live, and it has nothing to do with people.

B · Digital humans & avatars $11–13B

2026 market · forecasts diverge 3× by 2035

Video likeness generation, and genuinely commercial: Synthesia at $146M ARR raised $200M at a $4B valuation (GV, Nvidia); HeyGen reported $95M ARR in Sept 2025. A talking head that looks like you is a solved, sold product. It just doesn't know anything about you.

C · Person replicas unsized

No analyst firm sizes this segment

The actual "digital twin of you." Total disclosed venture funding across every pure-play startup in it: roughly $84M. Gartner named replicating employees a top 2026 CHRO trend, and still puts widespread digital-twin-of-customer adoption 5–10 years out.

Read-through

The third bar is deliberately not the same unit as the first two, because there is no revenue figure to put there. That mismatch is the finding. When 2026 estimates for the same "digital twin market" range from $26.4B to $39.8B and 2035 CAGRs from 30% to 46% across seven firms, those are extrapolations, not measurements, and none of them measure what Viven and Delphi sell. Size this category off capital concentration and adjacent ARR, not off a TAM slide. Two demand signals are checkable: 95% of HR leaders surveyed by Gartner are piloting or running generative AI and 80%+ intend to deploy agentic AI within a year; and behaviour-simulation twins are aiming at the ~$140B market-research industry, where one focus group still costs $5,000–$20,000.

Confidence: High on funding and ARR figures (primary announcements). Low on all TAM/CAGR figures: vendor-commissioned research, three-fold disagreement between firms, inconsistent category boundaries. Treat every dollar figure in the first two columns as directional only.

03 Architecture Viven · Delphi · Tavus, deconstructed

Nobody is training a model of you.

"A customized LLM for each employee." "A 3D model of your mind." Both are marketing. Underneath, every production system in this category does the same thing: it grounds a shared frontier model in per-person structured retrieval. Fine-tuning and RL are explicitly experimental at Viven; Delphi states it does not train on user content at all.

L7
Governancethe part enterprises actually buy
RBAC, full audit trails visible to the twin's owner as a social deterrent, PII withholding, on-prem / private VPC / BYOC deployment. Viven is sold on this layer, not the model layer.
L6
Embodimentoptional, and bought rather than built
ElevenLabs voice from a 30–60s sample. Tavus CVI for real-time video: Phoenix-4 hybrid Gaussian-diffusion at 40fps/1080p, Raven-1 perception, Sparrow-1 turn-taking at ~40ms granularity, sub-600ms end-to-end. Delphi is a Tavus customer. Viven ships none of it: text only, deliberately.
L5
Generationa shared, off-the-shelf LLM
Delphi runs Llama 3.3 70B on Cerebras at ~2,100 tok/s. Viven's model is undisclosed. Tavus is bring-your-own via any OpenAI-compatible endpoint. None of the three trains a language model on a person.
L4
Retrieval + authorizationthe real moat
Delphi: Pinecone, one namespace per mind. 100M+ vectors across 12,000+ namespaces, <100ms P95, ~20 qps. Viven: authorization enforced inside retrieval, via "pairwise context," where a VP's twin answers the CEO differently than a board member, because the graph decides what is reachable before the model sees anything.
L3
Person modelwhere "personality" actually lives
A temporal knowledge graph, not weights. Delphi's DMA1 is a self-adaptive temporally-indexed graph capturing how views evolve. Viven builds a dynamic per-employee graph from weak signals of expertise, a direct lineage from Eightfold's skills inference.
L2
Ingest & normalizethe unglamorous bottleneck
Transcription (AssemblyAI, with speaker ID, since most uploaded content is audio or video), chunking, embedding, entity resolution. Delphi's Interview Mode has an agent interrogate the person to fill gaps: a data-acquisition trick, not a model trick, and the honest admission that most people don't have enough corpus.
L1
Sourceseverything downstream is capped here
Enterprise: Gmail/Outlook, Slack/Teams, Drive/SharePoint, Jira, Confluence, Salesforce, meeting transcripts. Creator: YouTube, podcasts, books, Notion, blogs. Tavus needs only 1–2 minutes of video for the face. The face was never the hard part.
The finding

Fidelity comes from retrieval and graph structure, not from a bespoke model. Tavus is the only company here training proprietary foundation models (Phoenix, Raven, Sparrow, Hummingbird), and every one of them renders or perceives a face. They know nothing about the person. The knowledge is always RAG.

The consequence

If the model isn't the moat, the moat is (a) ingestion access, (b) graph quality, (c) the authorization layer. Two of those three are, precisely and without modification, what a company brain is. That is not a coincidence, and it is the whole of slide 06.

Caveat: Viven has published no engineering blog, whitepaper or patent; the pairwise-context mechanism is described only in a single founder interview. Tavus's latency and benchmark figures are vendor-reported. Delphi's retrieval numbers come from a Pinecone customer case study. Treat L4 for Viven as claimed architecture, not verified architecture. Update, Sep 2026: two Viven engineering posts (Dec 2025) partially supersede this caveat. L4 retrieval is now verified and the enterprise planner model is named. A second product, AskSila, runs on a disjoint vendor stack. See section 07 (press 7).
04 Employee use cases What is sold vs what is evidenced

Six use cases. One proof point.

Slide 02 compresses Viven to one line, "cross-team querying when someone is unavailable." That is one of six use cases it sells. Breadth is not evidence: every outcome claim in this category traces back to a single named account, and that account evidences only the first two.

01
Scale expertsthe headline
Reference deployment A teammate queries an expert's twin when that person is unavailable or overbooked. Confirmed verbatim in the launch release: twins "answer questions from teammates when they're unavailable."
02
Continuity and handoverthe strongest story
Reference deployment Institutional memory survives a role change. A Genpact Chief of Staff describes a predecessor's twin turning a months-long handover into days. This is the same job Sensay sells as "AI offboarding."
03

Claimed in primary source Twins "brief users before meetings, distill conversations across channels," verbatim from the 15 Oct 2025 release. A first-party claim rather than marketing copy, but no deployment evidences it.

05

No named account One coherent twin over a department's or account team's knowledge, so a CEO can query a global account team before a meeting. A first-class product surface with no customer named against it.

Least evidenced "Turns conversation into action, triggering updates, approvals, handoffs." The one use case that leaves retrieval behind, which is where durable value would sit, and the one with the least behind it.

The finding · convergence, not differentiation

All five converge on one primitive: capture tacit knowledge, then let other people query it. Slide 02 files onboarding under Sensay and IgniteTech, yet Viven's best testimonial is a handover story. Nobody owns a use case. They own an ingestion surface and a buyer.

The consequence · evidence is the scarce input

Two surfaces have a reference deployment, one has a first-party claim, three have neither. The category's only repeated hard metric is a deployment speed, not an outcome. The first credible outcome number here is worth more than the next funding round.

Evidence note: the eight-week Genpact rollout is the only widely repeated hard metric in this category, and it carries no published outcome data. The WSJ piece Viven features quantifies Reid Hoffman's twin and Greif's CHRO twin (~3,300 employee interactions); neither is a Viven deployment. Do not read those figures as Viven's.
05 The buyer Who signs, who blocks

Everyone files this under HR. Nobody sells it there.

The lineage is HR tech: Viven was incubated at Eightfold, the loudest analyst voice is Josh Bersin, and Gartner filed replicating employees as a 2026 CHRO trend. Yet across nine named customer voices, the launch release and the entire job board, there is not one HR buyer.

01
Economic buyersigns the cheque
CEO and the executive office, released through a Chief of Staff, COO or transformation officer. The release opens on the CEO's problem verbatim: "every CEO faces the same existential challenge."
02
Championdrives it internally
Chief of Staff to the CEO is the archetype, and the most vivid testimonial on the site belongs to one. Then the CEO directly, then line-of-business leaders once it expands past the leadership tier.
03
Technical approvercan stop the deal
CIO, transformation officer, enterprise architecture, security. They vet VPC and on-prem, RBAC and audit trails. Foundation Capital names the gate: "a product that IT departments will approve."
04
Economic validatorbuilds the case
CFO. The ROI testimonial is a CFO's, and it is framed in cost terms rather than capability terms: "faster decisions, fewer tools, less support overhead."
05
End usersnot the buyer
Employees whose twins are built, and those querying colleagues' twins. Note the asymmetry: the people supplying the training data are not the people buying, which is exactly where the consent problem originates.
06
Blockersthe real deal risk
Legal, privacy, security, and in the EU works councils. Twins built from mail and chat raise consent, likeness and IP ownership, and nobody has answered who owns a twin after the person leaves.
Evidence · the testimonial roster

Nine named voices. Zero from HR.

Counted off viven.ai: three CEOs or founder-CEOs, a Chief of Staff, two IT and transformation leaders, a CFO, a Chief Growth Officer, an analyst co-founder. For a company spun out of an HR-tech unicorn, that absence is a positioning decision, not an oversight.

Service-shaped tools sit with IT, learning-shaped tools sit with HR. Viven sits higher, at the leadership tier, with IT approving. Gartner expects blended HR-IT teams at 30% of organisations by 2029. That would move this. It has not moved yet.

Why it lands at the CEO, not the CHRO

An HR buyer purchases a workforce programme: rollout, adoption, policy. This is sold as executive leverage instead, top-down, and the reference deployment is a leadership rollout rather than a company-wide one. The only GTM role on the job board is one Account Executive, skills listed as B2B sales and enterprise software. No CHRO mandate appears anywhere.

Tripwires that would falsify this

A "for HR leaders" page appearing; GTM hires with a CHRO mandate; sponsorship of HR Tech, UNLEASH or SHRM; or CHRO testimonials joining the roster. Any one means the buyer is moving to HR. The opposite signal, compliance sales engineers and published SOC 2 / DPA collateral, would confirm the executive-plus-IT centre is permanent.

Caveat: nearly all of this is first-party, from Viven's site, its 15 Oct 2025 release and investor commentary, so it evidences marketed positioning, not where budget provably sits. Viven is post-seed and its own AE listing says the ICP is still being defined, so this buyer is a moving target. Round lead is reported both ways: the release names four investors without designating one, while Foundation Capital states it led.
06 Digital twins vs Company brain The verdict

Same machine. Different unit of indexing.

A digital twin is a company brain with one extra column on the index and a persona on the output. Around 80% of the build is identical, and the 20% that differs is not code. It is consent.

Digital twin

Indexed by person

  • Answers "what would Alice say?"
  • First person, tone-matched
  • Consent per individual, revocable
  • Bought by a CHRO or exec sponsor
  • Priced per person twinned
  • Failure = misrepresenting a human
~80%

of the stack is literally the same code

  • Connectors and ingestion
  • Transcription, chunking, embedding
  • Knowledge graph construction
  • Permission-aware retrieval
  • Citation and grounding discipline
  • Shared frontier LLM + audit trail
Company brain

Indexed by organization

  • Answers "what does the company know?"
  • Neutral, institutional voice
  • Consent granted once by IT, at org level
  • Bought by a CIO or knowledge ops
  • Priced per org seat
  • Failure = a wrong answer
Could both be one product? Yes, and one of them already is

Viven's headline feature is not a twin feature. "Pairwise context and privacy" is a company brain whose retrieval is authorization-aware, wearing a person as its interface. Strip the persona and you have Glean with a better permission model. Add a person axis and a tone layer to a brain and you have shipped a twin. What does not merge is consent: an org can grant IT access to its wiki, but it cannot grant your voice. AB 2602 is the only law on the books, there is no federal framework, and Gartner's analysts are floating NIL-style royalties. That gap is the product boundary, not the architecture.

Where GBrain / company-brain-ui sits

The prototype makes exactly two claims: a citation per claim, stopping where the evidence stops, and permissions in the database rather than a post-retrieval filter. Those are the two claims Viven raised $35M on, minus the person axis. The teammate switcher, where Alice, Bob and Carol ask one question and get three answers with the out-of-scope source named rather than silently dropped, is that same demo, already built. The twin is one GROUP BY away; the citation discipline was the harder half.

The call

01

Build the brain. Ship the twin as a view of it.

The brain is the substrate and the only layer with proven willingness to pay ($300M ARR at Glean). The twin is what makes a permission model legible in ten seconds: a marketing asset, not an architecture.

02

Don't build embodiment.

Voice and video are API calls to ElevenLabs and Tavus, and Delphi, the most consumer-facing player here, buys both. Tavus at ~$8M ARR against Synthesia's $146M says the money isn't in real-time faces anyway.

03

The open wedge is consent, not retrieval.

Nobody has shipped "the brain belongs to the company, the twin belongs to the person" as a primitive: revocation, portability on exit, an audit log the individual owns. Uare.ai gestures at it; no enterprise player has built it.

04

The risk: retrieval alone is one quarter of work for Glean.

If the twin is only a view of the brain, an incumbent ships it in a quarter. Copilot Memory is GA, Moveworks and Sana are absorbed, Glean compounds at 89%. Build what a seat-priced, org-indexed incumbent structurally cannot copy.

Bottom line: one product, two surfaces, is the right build. But the differentiator is the half nobody is building: the individual's side of the permission boundary. Everything else in this deck is commodity within eighteen months.

Enterprise query sequence

All participants verified. Ordering is the finding.

1 Asker sends query directed at Twin(B)
2 Scope resolved before plan generation
3 Audit log written before plan generation
4 Planner (gpt-4.1) emits 1-4 step plan
5 Executor retrieves derived chunks within scope
6 Synthesis returns twin response
7 Twin owner can view full query history

Steps 2 and 3 before 4 is the load-bearing constraint. Scope and audit precede the model.

The finding

Two independently published subprocessor disclosures, one dated June 2025 and the other August 2026, share zero vendors. The enterprise system runs on AWS, Azure, and OpenAI. AskSila runs on Vercel, Supabase, Anthropic, and Voyage AI. On the current evidence these are genuinely separate systems, not one backend with different packaging.

The consequence

The two-stack finding rests on a 14-month gap between the two documents. If the enterprise subprocessor notice were refreshed and named Anthropic, Voyage, or Vercel, the determination could move back toward a shared backend. The finding is falsifiable, and this section states how.

Caveat: this reconstruction uses public sources only, weighted toward legal disclosures, engineering posts and live DOM artefacts over marketing copy. The pairwise authorization mechanism remains described only in founder interviews and the launch release, so L4 authorization stays unverified even though L4 retrieval no longer is. No SOC 2 attestation, status page, public repository or patent was found. Nothing here is sourced to a private conversation, a customer, or a non-public document.
